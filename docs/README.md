# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the core project management guidance for OctoAcme. The goal is to provide a shared, repeatable operating model for cross-functional work so teams can move from idea to execution with clear ownership, consistent communication, and measurable outcomes.

## Overview of OctoAcme project management processes

OctoAcme follows a lifecycle that begins with initiation and ends with retrospective and continuous improvement. During initiation, teams validate the business need, define success metrics, and create a lightweight one-pager covering the problem, stakeholders, timeline, risks, and resource needs. Once the initiative is approved, planning turns the concept into a prioritized backlog, milestone plan, and clear definition of done. Execution then focuses on delivering small, testable increments with ongoing coordination, feedback, and dependency management.

The process depends on distinct but collaborative roles. Product managers define customer and business outcomes and prioritize the roadmap. Project managers coordinate delivery plans, schedules, risks, and stakeholder communication. Developers build and test the work, while QA validates acceptance criteria and release readiness. This structure helps teams keep work aligned and reduces ambiguity around ownership.

Communication is treated as a core management practice. OctoAcme uses daily standups to surface progress and blockers, weekly PM and product alignment meetings to review risks and decisions, and milestone-based updates for stakeholders. Escalation follows a clear path from the team to the PM, then the Product Lead, and potentially sponsor-level intervention for business-impacting issues. Risk management is tracked through a risk register so dependencies and mitigation actions remain visible across the project.

Quality is built into delivery rather than added at the end. Teams are expected to define acceptance criteria, maintain a clear definition of done, and run the appropriate unit, integration, and end-to-end checks where relevant. CI and security scans are used before merge, and releases require smoke tests, deployment validation, rollback planning, and stakeholder communication. Retrospectives capture what went well, what could improve, and what actions will close the loop.
